# Intake: fields to collect before building personas

Gather these before profiling segments. Pull what you can from the user's
request, ask for the essentials that are missing, and label assumptions where you
use a placeholder. Each field is annotated with what it drives.

## Campaign basics

- **Candidate name.** Used in sample lines and messenger framing.
- **Office.** Sets which issues land and how formal the personas read.
- **District or jurisdiction.** Anchors the geography and local concerns that
  separate one segment from another.

## The electorate

- **Voter universe and available data.** What the campaign actually has: turnout
  history, party registration, vote-by-mail propensity, age, geography, or even
  just what organizers hear at the doors. Drives how grounded (versus
  hypothetical) each persona can be.
- **Geographic areas or precincts of interest.** Where the votes are. Often the
  cleanest way to define a segment.
- **Known segments of interest.** Any groups the campaign already wants to reach
  (for example, lapsed supporters, new registrants, parents). Used as a starting
  list before adding from the segment library.

## Message inputs

- **Top issues.** The campaign's priorities, in order. These supply each
  persona's motivating issue.
- **Tone.** Professional, populist, or neighborly. Shapes the sample lines.
- **Candidate do-not-say.** Words, claims, or positions the candidate will not
  use. Feeds every persona's do-not-say field.

## Reach

- **Channels available.** Which of door, phone, SMS, mail, and digital the
  campaign can actually run. A best channel the campaign cannot use is not a
  recommendation.
- **Messengers available.** Surrogates, local leaders, or institutions willing to
  vouch. Drives the best messenger field.

These personas are working hypotheses to test against the campaign's own data and
field experience, not fixed facts about voters.
