# Objection handling

Voters push back. The goal is not to win an argument, it is to keep a short,
respectful conversation moving toward the ask, or to disengage cleanly when that
is the right call. Stay warm, stay brief, and never talk over a voter.

## The bridge: acknowledge, redirect, reask

Three steps, used in order:

1. **Acknowledge.** Reflect what the voter said so they feel heard. One short
   sentence. Do not argue with it.
2. **Redirect.** Offer one relevant point: a reason, a fact, or a quick story
   tied to the campaign's top issues.
3. **Reask.** Return to the original question or ask. Make it easy to answer.

Use it once or twice, not endlessly. If the voter is clearly done, move to the
data and close.

## Voter objections

**"Not interested."**
- Acknowledge: "Totally fair, I will be quick."
- Redirect: one sentence on why this race matters locally.
- Reask: "Before I let you go, can I ask which issue matters most to you this
  year?"

**"I have already decided."**
- Acknowledge: "Great, it helps us to know where folks stand."
- Redirect: do not push. If they will share, note it.
- Reask: "Mind if I ask who you are leaning toward, so we have an accurate count?"
  Then capture the support score and stop.

**"You are all the same."**
- Acknowledge: "I hear that a lot, and I get the frustration."
- Redirect: one concrete, specific contrast on a top issue, stated factually.
- Reask: "Given that, where would you put yourself, leaning for or against?"

**"I am too busy right now."**
- Acknowledge: "No problem, I will not keep you."
- Redirect: offer the single most important question or a callback.
- Reask: "Is now bad, or could a quick text later work better?" Capture the best
  follow-up method.

**"I would rather not say."**
- Acknowledge: "Completely understand, no pressure at all."
- Redirect: none. Respect the privacy.
- Reask: at most, "Is there an issue you would want the campaign to hear about?"
  Record as undecided or refused and move on.

**Hostile.**
- Do not bridge. See "When to disengage" below.

## Volunteer recruitment objections

**"I do not have time."**
- Acknowledge: "Totally understand, everyone is stretched."
- Redirect: name the smallest real option (a single two-hour shift, or texting
  from your couch).
- Reask: "Could you do one short shift before election day?"

**"What would I even be doing?"**
- Acknowledge: "Good question, it is simpler than people expect."
- Redirect: describe one role plainly (knock a turf with a list and an app, or
  make calls from a script just like this one, with training first).
- Reask: "Want me to get you set up for a first shift this week?"

**"I am not political enough for that."**
- Acknowledge: "You do not need to be, honestly."
- Redirect: the best volunteers are neighbors talking to neighbors; the campaign
  brings the script and the training.
- Reask: "Would you be up for trying one shift with someone experienced?"

## When to disengage

Some responses end the contact. These override the bridge. Never bridge past a
clear no or a request not to be contacted.

- **Hostility.** If the voter is angry, abusive, or escalating, do not engage or
  argue. "I understand, I will let you go. Thanks for your time." End the contact
  and mark the record so it is handled appropriately.
- **Do-not-contact request.** If the voter asks not to be contacted, by any
  channel, honor it immediately. Do not reask and do not bridge. Confirm briefly,
  record the request in the voter file or CRM, and remove them from future
  outreach on that channel.

Disengaging well protects the campaign and the voter relationship. A clean exit
and an accurate record are a successful contact.
