# Source logging

A vulnerability memo is only as good as its sources. Log every source before it
enters the memo, and rate it so the campaign knows how hard it can lean on each
finding. If something cannot be logged to a real public source, it does not go in
the memo; it goes under follow-up research needed.

## How to log a source

For each item, record:

- **Claim:** the specific fact the source supports, stated plainly.
- **Source:** the publication, body, or document (for example, "City Council
  minutes," "Associated Press," "FEC filing").
- **Date:** when the source was published or the action occurred.
- **Where to find it:** the link, archive, docket number, or filing ID so anyone
  can re-check it.
- **Type:** see the categories below.
- **Reliability:** see the rating scale below.

## What counts as public record

Use material that is public and verifiable, including:

- Recorded votes and legislative history.
- On-the-record public statements, speeches, and interviews.
- Campaign finance and financial disclosure filings.
- Court records and official documents that are public.
- Government reports and official meeting minutes.
- Reporting from named, reputable news outlets.

## What to exclude

- Private information about anyone, including family and personal life.
- Rumor, hearsay, or anything that cannot be traced to a source.
- Anything obtained improperly or through unauthorized access.
- Claims the user wants included but cannot source. These are leads to verify,
  not findings.

## Reliability rating

Rate each source so the memo can weight findings:

- **Primary public record (strongest):** the actual vote, filing, document, or
  on-the-record quote.
- **Reputable secondary reporting:** named, credible news organizations reporting
  the fact.
- **Partisan or advocacy source:** useful as a lead, but corroborate with a
  primary record before relying on it.
- **Unverified:** a single weak source or an uncited claim. Do not use; log as
  follow-up.

When a finding rests on anything below a primary record or reputable reporting,
flag it as needing corroboration in the memo's risk level section.
