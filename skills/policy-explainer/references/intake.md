# Intake: fields to collect before writing the explainer

Gather these before writing. Pull what you can from the user's request, ask for
the essentials that are missing, and never invent the candidate's position or its
details. Each field is annotated with what it drives.

## Where these fields come from

If a `campaigns/<name>.md` profile exists, reuse the shared fields from it and do not
re-ask. Run the `campaign-setup` skill to create one if there is none.

- From the profile: candidate name, office, district or jurisdiction, party,
  opponent(s), top issues, default tone, and key dates (election date and early-vote
  window). The profile's notes section may also hold do-not-say language, a slogan,
  or endorsements.
- Ask per task (not stored in the profile): the specific policy or position (as the
  candidate states it), the goal of the policy, the supporting facts or data, the
  local impact specifics, who benefits and who pays, the frame and values to lead
  with, the reading level or audience, and the opponent's contrasting position.

The field notes below still apply; the profile simply pre-fills the shared ones.

## The position

- **The policy or position.** What the candidate actually supports, stated as the
  candidate states it. The basis of everything. Ask if unclear; do not guess.
- **Goal of the policy.** What it is meant to achieve. Anchors the why voters
  should care section.
- **Supporting facts or data.** The proof points that back the position. Use what
  the user provides; flag anything unverified.

## Impact

- **Local impact specifics.** What this means in this district: a place, a number,
  a group of people. Drives the local impact section.
- **Who benefits and who pays (if relevant).** Helps anticipate objections
  honestly.

## Frame (provided, not assumed)

- **Frame and values to use.** The lens the campaign wants to lead with (for
  example, cost, fairness, freedom, safety, opportunity). This is required input;
  if it is missing, ask before writing rather than choosing a default.
- **Tone.** Professional, populist, or neighborly.
- **Reading level or audience.** Who must understand it on first read. Keeps the
  language plain enough.

## Context

- **Opponent's position (optional).** The contrasting view, for the objections and
  rebuttals. Stated fairly.
- **Candidate and office.** For voice and scope.

This intake supplies the position and the frame. It does not decide the
candidate's policy or pick a frame on its own.
