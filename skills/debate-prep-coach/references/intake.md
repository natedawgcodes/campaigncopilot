# Intake: fields to collect before debate prep

Gather these before building the prep set. Pull what you can from the user's
request, ask for the essentials that are missing, and never invent a candidate
position. Each field is annotated with what it drives.

## Where these fields come from

If a `campaigns/<name>.md` profile exists, reuse the shared fields from it and do not
re-ask. Run the `campaign-setup` skill to create one if there is none.

- From the profile: candidate name, office, district or jurisdiction, party,
  opponent(s), top issues, default tone, and key dates (election date and early-vote
  window). The profile's notes section may also hold do-not-say language, a slogan,
  or endorsements.
- Ask per task (not stored in the profile): the debate format and rules, the time
  limits per answer, the moderator or outlet, the candidate's actual positions on
  the issues, the campaign's core message, the candidate's own vulnerabilities, the
  opponent's likely attacks and record, and the audience in the room.

The field notes below still apply; the profile simply pre-fills the shared ones.

## Candidate and race

- **Candidate name.** Used throughout; answers are written in the candidate's
  voice.
- **Office.** Sets which issues and which level of detail are expected.
- **Opponent or opponents.** Drives the hostile questions and the attack and
  response matrix.

## Format

- **Debate format and rules.** Town hall, podium debate, forum, or interview;
  whether there are rebuttals; audience or no audience. Shapes tactics.
- **Time limits per answer.** Drives the 30-second and 2-minute outlines and how
  tight each answer must be.
- **Moderator or outlet.** Helps anticipate the likely questions and tone.

## Substance

- **Top issues and the candidate's actual positions.** The core of the prep. If a
  position is unknown, ask; do not guess.
- **Campaign core message.** The destination every answer bridges toward.
- **Candidate's own vulnerabilities.** Where the candidate is weakest. Drives the
  hostile questions and the truth lines that defuse them.
- **Opponent's likely attacks and record.** What the opponent will say and the
  contrasts available in response.

## Delivery

- **Audience.** Who is in the room and watching. Shapes tone and examples.
- **Tone.** Professional, populist, or neighborly, matched to the candidate and
  the moment.

This intake does not generate positions the candidate has not taken. It prepares
the candidate to defend and advance their real positions.
