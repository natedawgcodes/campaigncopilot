# Intake: fields to collect before building the message set

Gather these before writing. Pull what you can from the user's request, ask for
the essentials that are missing, and label placeholders clearly. Each field is
annotated with what it drives.

## Where these fields come from

If a `campaigns/<name>.md` profile exists, reuse the shared fields from it and do not
re-ask. Run the `campaign-setup` skill to create one if there is none.

- From the profile: candidate name, office, district or jurisdiction, party,
  opponent(s), top issues, default tone, and key dates (election date and early-vote
  window). The profile's notes section may also hold do-not-say language, a slogan,
  or endorsements.
- Ask per task (not stored in the profile): the candidate's biographical facts, key
  accomplishments, why they are running, the values they lead with, any slogan or
  tagline, the first-priority audience, and any endorsements.

The field notes below still apply; the profile simply pre-fills the shared ones.

## Candidate and race

- **Candidate name.** Used throughout and in the bios.
- **Office.** Sets scope, tone, and which accomplishments are relevant.
- **District or jurisdiction.** Anchors local ties and the voter's frame of
  reference.

## The person

- **Biographical facts.** Background, career, family, local roots, and service.
  Feeds both bios and the stump pitch.
- **Key accomplishments.** Specific, verifiable wins or experience. Supply the
  proof points; do not invent them.
- **Why running.** The candidate's own reason, in their words if available. This
  is the heart of the core message.
- **Values.** The few principles the candidate leads with.

## Message inputs

- **Top three issues.** In order. They populate the palm card bullets, the stump
  pitch, and the issue framing across pieces.
- **Contrast with opponent (optional).** Where the candidate differs, stated
  factually. Used lightly and only where it strengthens the message.
- **Slogan or tagline (optional).** If one exists, keep it consistent everywhere.

## Voice and audience

- **Tone.** Professional, populist, or neighborly. Must read consistently across
  every format.
- **Target audience.** Who the message must reach first. Shapes emphasis and
  examples.
- **Endorsements (optional).** Named supporters that add credibility to the bio
  and palm card.

This intake supplies the facts; it does not fabricate biography, accomplishments,
or endorsements. Confirm anything used as a proof point.
